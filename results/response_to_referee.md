# Response to the referee report on project C3

*Manuscript (outline stage): "Syndrome-only leakage tracking for matching decoders" (formerly "Soft leakage tracking from syndromes alone…"). Date: 18 October 2026. All numbers below come from code run in this project; the evidence file for each point is named in brackets. Plain language on purpose.*

## 0. What changed because of this review, in one paragraph

We did the three things the referee said would decide the paper: a real-device test (3.6), stronger baselines (3.3, 3.4), and the statistics/derivation/amendment hygiene (3.7, 3.8, 3.11), plus a check of the six citations (§2). The baselines and statistics strengthen the simulation result (belief matching alone captures only 0.13 to 0.28 of the oracle gain; the per-shot posterior adds ≈ 0.6 on top; all 153 tracker-vs-A comparisons survive Holm; Fieller and bootstrap intervals agree to 0.003). **The real-device test, however, is a null.** On the Google 2023 d = 3/d = 5 syndromes (the only public surface-code data without per-cycle data-qubit leakage removal) soft leakage weights give no gain, they are 0.7 to 1.4 % *worse* at d = 3 and null at d = 5, and a simulator fitted to each experiment predicts the same null, because on that device perfect leakage flags would remove only 2.9 % (d = 3) or 9.9 % (d = 5) of the logical error per round. We therefore agree with the referee's closing sentence: the paper's value is decided by realism and real data. The honest paper is now a **scope paper**: it states when syndrome-only leakage tracking helps (leakage a large share of the budget, background detection fraction ≲ 5 %, episodes lasting several rounds), shows with controls that in that regime the gain is real and comes from per-shot information, and reports that no public dataset is in that regime today. We no longer propose PRX Quantum. [real_data_report.md, baselines_report.md, statistics_addendum.md, prior_art_verification.md]

## 1. Summary and §2 novelty

We accept the referee's description of the contribution and adopt it as the novelty statement: a per-shot, multi-round leakage posterior from syndromes only, converted to soft weights in an unmodified MWPM core, with a measurement of the recovered fraction of the oracle gain against the natural controls. Claim 2 (soft beats hard) will be presented as a quantified confirmation, not a finding.

**The six citations were read** [prior_art_verification.md]. None kills novelty. 2610.03869 (Lange et al., modular GNN-RNN decoder) and Remm et al. (PRR 8, 013044 (2026), arXiv 2502.17722, the referee's "generalised Spitz" paper) must be cited as adjacent; Remm et al. use one *static* weight set estimated from the whole dataset, nothing per shot, and name leakage-aware decoding as future work. 2609.10666, 2606.19482 and He et al. (PRL 135, 260601, DOI 10.1103/rqkg-dw31, verified) are scope limits: hardware or circuit-level leakage removal every cycle. 2601.09921 is a neural-decoder throughput paper, irrelevant to the claim. A web-level scan found no 2025 to 2026 paper feeding a syndrome-inferred leakage posterior into matching weights (hardware heralds, analog-readout flags, and dataset-averaged weights only). Caveat declared: four of the six were read at abstract + fragment level because arxiv.org was proxy-blocked in our sandbox; Remm et al. was read in full.

## 2. Major concerns

**3.1 Noise model circularity.** Agreed for the HMM. Partly answered for the CNN: it was tested off-cell (trained at the centre cell, tested at four other (p_L, τ) cells: f = 0.62 to 0.75 vs matched 0.66 to 0.74) and at ρ ≠ training ρ (0.61 at ρ = 0.25, 0.57 at ρ = 0.7). The real-data test is the strongest answer to circularity, and it is a null (see 3.6). Measure-qubit leakage and transport remain unmodelled; we now list them as required before any resubmission rather than "after".

**3.2 Headline vs realistic regime.** Accepted in full, and the real data make it sharper than the referee put it. Background scan at fixed (p_L, τ) = (1e-3, 8), d = 5: p = 1e-3 / 2e-3 / 3e-3 gives detection fraction 2.4 / 6.2 / 8.5 %, oracle LER reduction 76 / 47 / 29 %, CNN soft 56 / 22 / 8 % (12 % with 4× training data). The Google 2023 device has detection fraction 14 to 17 %: fitted oracle reduction 2.9 % (d = 3), 9.9 % (d = 5); tracker 0 %. The abstract will lead with these absolute numbers and the regime statement; f becomes a secondary, mechanism-level quantity. ΔΛ at the p = 1e-3 centre cell (Λ₅/₇: 1.40 → 2.72 with CNN, 4.11 oracle) will be reported as a simulation-only number with the leakage share (95 %) printed beside it. [c3_paper_readiness_report.md §5, g5_pscan_figure.png, real_data_report.md §5]

**3.3 Baselines too weak.** Done, on identical shots [baselines_report.md]:

| decoder (d = 5 centre cell, 1e5 shots) | f [95 %] |
|---|---|
| belief matching (Higgott, leakage-free DEM) | 0.17 [0.13, 0.20] |
| count heuristic (adjacent detection events in a 4-round window → logistic → same soft rule) | 0.35 [0.32, 0.38] |
| best static data-fitted DEM, A2'learn (replaces the Spitz straw man) | 0.42 [0.39, 0.45] |
| HMM soft | 0.51 [0.48, 0.53] |
| CNN soft, lag 2 | 0.72 [0.70, 0.74] |
| belief matching + CNN posterior as per-shot priors | 0.77 [0.75, 0.80] |

Belief matching alone: 0.24 (d = 3), 0.17 (d = 5), 0.13 (d = 7); with the CNN posterior folded into its priors: 0.69, 0.77, 0.85. Its leakage-free gain (29 % relative) is of the same kind as its gain under leakage (14 %): it handles Y-type correlations, it does not track leakage. The sentence requested by the referee: *belief matching alone captures 0.17 of the oracle gain; CNN soft weights add 0.61 on top.* An end-to-end neural decoder of comparable compute was **not** run (compute); the modularity argument will be stated as a design choice with the belief-matching + posterior result as its evidence, not as a claim of superiority over end-to-end decoders.

**3.4 A2 is a straw man.** Accepted. We replaced it by two fair static controls fitted on leaky calibration data: A2'corr (pairwise-correlation estimate restricted to the circuit graph, invalid edges kept at their circuit value instead of clipped, the clipping was the pathology) gives f = 0.06 to 0.11 at d = 5; A2'learn (one multiplicative factor per edge class, chosen to minimise logical error on 2e4 calibration shots, i.e. a Remm-style dataset-averaged weight set with the decoding error as objective) gives 0.37 to 0.47 at d = 5 and 0.59 at d = 7. The learned factors make time-like Z-edges 3 to 20× more probable relative to space-like edges, the static decoder learns that "measurement-like" errors are commoner than the circuit model says, and that is worth ≈ 0.4 of the oracle gain. The remaining ≈ 0.3 is per-shot information. The old A2 result will be kept only as a one-sentence warning about clipping. Remm et al. will be cited for this control.

**3.5 Distance scaling underpowered.** Partly accepted. We will not claim "f grows with d" as a scaling law; we will report f(d = 3, 5, 7) with Fieller intervals and the 8/9 interval separations, note that the d = 7 CNN is train/test mismatched (conservative, the mismatch can only lower f, and at d = 5 mismatch cost ≤ 0.05), and run d = 9 for the HMM and the oracle on a larger machine before resubmission. d = 11 we decline: it adds little to a methods/scope paper. HMM state space: factorised per data qubit (4 states per qubit: (L_{t−1}, L_t)); it ignores correlations between neighbouring leaked qubits and shared-stabiliser ambiguity beyond two mean-field explaining-away iterations. This will be stated.

**3.6 No experimental data.** Done; this is the decisive result [real_data_report.md]. Dataset: Zenodo 6804040 (Google 2023), ten 25-round experiments, 50 000 shots each. Decoder A reproduces the dataset's shipped PyMatching predictions shot-by-shot (99.8 to 99.98 % at d = 3). The hardware detection fraction rises by +0.006 to +0.020 over 24 rounds while the device Pauli model is flat; fitting our leakage model to that rise gives p_L ≈ 2e-3, τ ≈ 8 at d = 5 (1.2 % of qubit-rounds leaked), consistent with the paper's own attribution. Result on the real syndromes, pooled:

| set | A4 static control | HMM soft | CNN soft lag 0 | CNN soft lag 2 | CNN hard |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| d = 3, 400 k shots, ε ratio vs A | 0.995 [0.990, 1.000] | 1.014 [1.006, 1.021] | 1.007 [1.000, 1.014] | 1.008 [1.001, 1.016] | 1.001 |

The flags are not random, after a flagged qubit-round the adjacent detectors fire 0.36 to 0.42 of the time in the next round vs 0.15 to 0.17 after an unflagged one, and the hardware flag rate equals the fitted simulator's within 0.03 percentage points, but at recall 0.08 to 0.15 and ≈ 30 % false positives they move too few weights correctly when the background detection fraction is 0.14 to 0.17. The fitted simulator, where the oracle exists, predicts f = 0.0 ± 0.4 for the soft decoders and an oracle gain of only 2.9 % / 9.9 %. This is the referee's "honest null explained by the regime", except that the explanation is not per-cycle DQLR (the 2023 device had none) but the noise level: leakage was a 3 to 10 % share of the logical error. The Willow 2025 data (DQLR every cycle) would be a second, scope-null test; it was not downloaded (5.7 to 112 GB) and is listed as future work with the exact protocol.

*Added after the real-data result, oracle gain vs distance at 2023-like noise (new cells G6, SI1000 p = 6e-3, p_L = 2e-3, τ = 8, 5e4 shots, detection fraction 0.145 to 0.164, 1.2 % of qubit-rounds leaked; simulation, oracle only):* the plain decoder sits at threshold (ε_A = 0.0213 / 0.0218 / 0.0219 / 0.0231 per round at d = 3 / 5 / 7 / 9) and perfect leakage flags would remove 9 / 16 / 23 / 27 % of the logical error. So on a 2023-class device the *available* gain does grow with distance, leakage is what keeps that device at threshold, but a syndrome-only tracker at that detection fraction recovers none of it (real-data result above; fitted-simulator f = 0.0 ± 0.4). This is the clearest form of the scope statement: the tracker needs a clean background, and the devices with clean backgrounds (Willow-class) remove leakage every cycle. [results/G6_*.json]

**3.7 Derivation of the soft-weight rule.** Done [derivation_soft_weight.md, in baselines_report.md]. Marginalising the binary leakage state gives exactly p_e = (1−π)p_e⁰ + π(p_e⁰ ⊕ q_e) = p_e⁰ ⊕ (π q_e), with q_e the flip probability of edge e given leakage (ρ for time-like edges of adjacent stabilisers, 2/3 for the X/Z return edges, ρ for the final edge). The approximations are listed: independence across edges (leakage flips several adjacent time-like edges jointly), independence across rounds, the return-probability proxy, double use of the syndrome, the 0.02 threshold, no new hyperedges. Sensitivity: scaling c by s ∈ {0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.25, 1.5} on time-like / return / final edges separately changes f by at most 0.06 (0.66 to 0.73), with s ≥ 1 marginally best on time-like edges, as expected when the independence approximation under-weights joint flips. The headline is not a tuned constant.

## 4. Revised abstract (draft)

Leakage out of the computational subspace produces correlated, multi-round errors in superconducting surface codes. We ask how much of the leakage-induced logical error a matching decoder can remove using only the syndrome history, with no hardware herald. A per-qubit, per-round leakage probability is inferred by a hidden-Markov tracker or a small causal convolutional network and converted, by exact marginalisation over the leakage state, into per-shot edge weights of an unmodified minimum-weight-perfect-matching decoder. In circuit-level simulations where leakage dominates the error budget (detection fraction 2 to 3 %, episodes of several rounds), the network tracker removes 50 to 60 % of the logical error per round at distance 5 (perfect flags: 76 %), more than belief matching (14 %) or the best static re-weighting learned from the same syndromes (≈ 35 %), and the gain comes from per-shot information. The benefit falls steeply with the background detection fraction: to 8 to 12 % at 8.5 % detection fraction and to zero on the public Google 2023 distance-3 and distance-5 data (14 to 17 % detection fraction), where even perfect leakage knowledge would remove only 3 to 10 % of the error. Syndrome-only leakage tracking is therefore a tool for low-noise devices without per-cycle leakage removal; on current public hardware data it gives no gain.
